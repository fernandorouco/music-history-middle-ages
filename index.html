MUSICAL ANALYSIS TEMPLATE

1. TECHNICAL AND SONIC ANALYSIS
1.1. Instrumentation:

  • Voices only (A cappella): Male / Female / Mixed / Choir?
  • Instruments only: Main instrument family / Soloist / Orchestra / Synthesized?
  • Mixed: Voices + Instruments.

1.2. Predominant Texture:

  • Monophony / Monophonic: A single melodic line (in unison or octaves).
  • Polyphony / Polyphonic: Multiple independent melodies moving simultaneously or in imitation (counterpoint).
  • Homophony / Homophonic: Multiple parts moving together in block chords with the same rhythm.
  • Melody and Accompaniment: A clear solo lead melody supported by an underlying chordal/instrumental accompaniment.

1.3. Rhythm and Tempo:

  • Free rhythm: No steady beat or meter (flexible, tied to breath or speech).
  • Strict / Measured rhythm: Clear, steady beat with an identifiable meter (duple / triple).
  • General Tempo: Slow (Lento) / Moderate (Moderato) / Fast (Allegro).
  • Tempo changes: Steady pulse / Accelerando / Ritardando.

1.4. Form / Basic Structure:

Organization:

  • Continuous (Through-composed): Follows the text without clear repeated sections.
  • Binary (AA or AB) / Ternary (ABA).
  • Rondo / Refrain (A - B - A - C - A -...).
  • Theme and Variations.
  • Popular Song Form: Intro / Verse / Chorus / Bridge / Solo / Build-Up / Drop / Outro / Coda.

Interaction:

Direct: The whole ensemble or soloist performs continuously from start to finish.
Alternating / Dialogue: Soloist vs. ensemble (Call and response / Responsorial) or two groups alternating.


2. CONTEXT, FUNCTION, AND LANGUAGE
2.1. Original Social Function:

  • Religious / Liturgical: Worship, prayer, church/temple service.
  • Courtly / Aristocratic: Palace dancing, private chamber music, display of status and power.
  • Popular / Secular Entertainment: Traditional folk dance, protest song, public celebration, commercial theatre, concert hall.

2.2. Text and Language:

  • Instrumental (no lyrics).
  • Vocal with text: Latin / Vernacular language (Spanish, French, English, Italian...)?

2.3. Connection to its Era:

Explain in 1–2 sentences what this music reflects about its historical period and why it sounds that way.(e.g., the importance of religion in the medieval era, Renaissance humanism, Baroque theatrical drama, Enlightenment order and balance, Hippie movement, British Punk movement...).
